It's called lead scoring. Let's scoring means giving
every lead a number so you can sort your list from the most promising to the least.
Two things go into that number.
The first is fit. Does this company look like your best customers?
Think about the industry, the size of the company,
the country, and the job title of the person you're talking to.
A marketing director at a 200 person software company might be a perfect fit.
A student downloading a free guide probably isn't.
The second is intent. Is this lead showing interest right now?
Did they visit your pricing page? Open your emails, ask for a demo, reply to a message?
Fit tells you who could buy. Intent tells you who might buy soon.
Put the two together and you get three groups.
Hot leads fit well and are active now. Call them today.
Warm leads fit but they're quiet or they're active but not a perfect fit.
Keep them warm with useful emails and check back in a couple of weeks.
Cold leads don't fit and aren't active. Don't spend your best hours on them.
Here's a simple way to start tomorrow. Take your CRM export.
Give each lead up to 50 points for fit and up to 50 for intent.
For fit, 10 points for the right industry, 10 for the right company size,
10 for a decision maker job title. For intent,
20 points for a visit to your pricing page, 20 for a demo request, 10 for a reply.
Anything over 70 is hot. 40 to 70 is warm. Below 40 is cold.
It won't be perfect but it will already be better than guessing.
Now three mistakes to avoid.
One, scoring only on fit. A perfect company that hasn't touched your website in a year is not hot.
Two, never updating the scores. A lead score should change when their behavior changes.
Three, hiding the reason. If a salesperson can't see why a lead is hot,
