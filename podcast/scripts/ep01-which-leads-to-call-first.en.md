# Episode 1 — Which leads should you call first?

- **Reel:** the part marked ✂️ (about 45 seconds) makes a good reel

Hi, and welcome to the CRM Scoring podcast. I'm [your name].

Every sales team has the same problem: too many leads and not enough hours. So someone has to decide who gets the first call. In most teams, that decision is made by gut feeling — or by whoever spoke loudest in the last meeting.

Today I want to show you a simpler way. It's called lead scoring.

Lead scoring means giving every lead a number, so you can sort your list from the most promising to the least. Two things go into that number.

The first is **fit**. Does this company look like your best customers? Think about the industry, the size of the company, the country, and the job title of the person you're talking to. A marketing director at a two-hundred-person software company might be a perfect fit. A student downloading a free guide probably isn't.

The second is **intent**. Is this lead showing interest right now? Did they visit your pricing page, open your emails, ask for a demo, reply to a message?

Fit tells you who *could* buy. Intent tells you who might buy *soon*.

Put the two together and you get three groups.

**Hot** leads fit well and are active now. Call them today.

**Warm** leads fit, but they're quiet — or they're active, but not a perfect fit. Keep them warm with useful emails, and check back in a couple of weeks.

**Cold** leads don't fit and aren't active. Don't spend your best hours on them.

✂️ *[Reel starts]*

Here's a simple way to start tomorrow. Take your CRM export. Give each lead up to fifty points for fit and up to fifty for intent.

For fit: ten points for the right industry, ten for the right company size, ten for a decision-maker job title.

For intent: twenty points for a visit to your pricing page, twenty for a demo request, ten for a reply.

Anything over seventy is hot. Forty to seventy is warm. Below forty is cold.

It won't be perfect — but it will already be better than guessing.

✂️ *[Reel ends]*

Now, three mistakes to avoid.

One: scoring only on fit. A perfect company that hasn't touched your website in a year is not hot.

Two: never updating the scores. A lead's score should change when their behaviour changes.

Three: hiding the reason. If a salesperson can't see *why* a lead is hot, they won't trust the number — and they'll go back to gut feeling.
